FALL/WINTER 2026 DIRECTION

Two forces are pulling against each other this season: quiet luxury — logo-free, fabric-first dressing in muted greige, navy, and stone — and a countercurrent of bold color returning to the runways after several quiet years. The elite read on both: run 90% quiet-luxury restraint and let exactly one piece carry the color. Never two.

Forest greenChestnut brownAnthracite greyBurgundyCamel / tanSlate navyOlive / sageTomato red (accent only)

Fabrics & pieces — cashmere, vicuña blends, merino wool, flannel, corduroy, moleskin, donegal or herringbone tweed. Ribbed cardigans, cable-knit crewnecks and turtlenecks worn a size relaxed, peacoats and topcoats, leather bombers, utility trench coats. The single easiest upgrade: a quarter-zip in camel, charcoal, or forest green — it sits between casual and put-together and works everywhere.

Silhouette — relaxed over slim. Wider trousers, and if the knit is oversized, the outer layer goes slimmer (and vice versa) — only one loose piece per outfit. One dominant pattern per look, windowpane or glen plaid, everything else solid.

Footwear — penny loafers for dress-to-weekend range, square toes overtaking round across loafers, Chelsea boots, and lace-ups. Slim-soled Chelsea boots for early fall, hiking-inspired lace-ups once it turns cold.

Accessories — medium-width ties in deep neutrals (brown, burgundy, navy, muted green) beat ultra-skinny. One signet ring or simple chain, never both. One excellent dark leather belt and wallet — cheap leather looks its best on day one, good leather gets better with use.

Reference points for cut and proportion: Loro Piana (outerwear and fabric), Brunello Cucinelli (tonal greige/navy/stone dressing), Zegna (sharper tailoring).
